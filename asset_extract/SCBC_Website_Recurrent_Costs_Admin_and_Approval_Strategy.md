# Sri Chandananda Buddhist College Website

## Professional Strategy for Recurrent Digital Costs, Administration & School Approval

**Document purpose:** Internal planning document for presenting and
managing the school website project professionally.

> **Important:** This document uses ethical persuasion and transparent
> business practices. It should not attempt to manipulate, pressure, or
> "brainwash" the Principal. The objective is to make the value,
> responsibilities, recurring costs, ownership, and benefits clear
> enough that the school can make an informed decision.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. The Core Situation

The website may be presented as a **low-cost / community-supported
digital initiative**, but the school should still understand that a
professional website has recurring operating costs.

The key principle is:

> **The website development can be contributed by a past pupil, while
> the school directly pays unavoidable third-party operating costs.**

This creates a practical model:

-   Past pupil contributes development/admin time.
-   School owns the digital assets.
-   School pays the necessary recurring service subscriptions.
-   The website remains operational even if the original developer is
    unavailable.
-   All accounts and ownership are documented.
-   Any future development or maintenance can be separately agreed.

This is much more professional than saying everything is permanently
"free."

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. How to Position the Proposal

Avoid saying:

> "I will make the website completely free."

Instead say:

> **"As a past pupil, I am prepared to contribute my development and
> initial administrative support to help establish the school's digital
> presence. The school would only need to cover the essential
> third-party service costs required to keep the system active."**

This distinction is important.

### Development contribution

You may voluntarily contribute:

-   Website design
-   Initial development
-   Initial setup
-   Initial deployment
-   Basic administration
-   Initial SEO setup
-   Basic analytics setup
-   Initial training

### School-funded operating costs

The school can pay directly for:

-   Domain
-   Google Workspace / school email
-   Any future paid software
-   Any future paid hosting/service required by the approved system

This keeps the proposal transparent.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. The Principal Should See the Website as an Institutional Asset

The discussion should not focus only on:

> "How much does a website cost?"

Instead, demonstrate what the school receives.

## Institutional benefits

A professional website can become:

-   The school's official digital identity
-   A permanent online information centre
-   A showcase of school achievements
-   A platform for events and activities
-   A place for admissions information
-   A digital archive of important milestones
-   A way for parents and past pupils to find verified information
-   A platform to showcase students' achievements
-   A professional reference point for external organisations
-   A foundation for future digital services

### The emotional value

The presentation can respectfully connect the project with school pride:

> **"Our school has achievements, history, teachers, students, past
> pupils and traditions worth documenting. A professional website gives
> the school a permanent public platform to present that identity."**

This is legitimate value communication---not pressure.

# 5. Why the School Should Pay the Workspace Cost

The strongest argument is **ownership and continuity**, not personal
benefit.

The school should not think:

> "We are paying the developer's email bill."

The school should think:

> **"We are maintaining the school's official digital administration
> system."**

The Google Workspace account can provide:

-   Professional school email
-   Google Drive
-   Shared documents
-   Calendar
-   Administrative communication
-   Account security
-   Centralised access
-   Institutional continuity

For example:

-   `principal@scbck.lk`
-   `office@scbck.lk`
-   `admin@scbck.lk`

The exact accounts should be approved by the school.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Important Ownership Principle

The school should ideally own the infrastructure.

### Recommended ownership

The following should be registered under school-controlled accounts
wherever practical:

-   Domain
-   Google Workspace
-   Cloudflare account
-   Google Analytics
-   Google Search Console
-   YouTube
-   Facebook/Meta page
-   Google Drive
-   Website source repository

The developer/past pupil can receive appropriate administrator or
developer access.

### Why?

This protects both sides.

If the developer stops working on the project:

> The school still owns its digital assets.

If the school changes administrators:

> The digital infrastructure can be transferred internally without
> rebuilding everything.

This is a professional institutional model.

# 7. How to Discuss Recurring Costs

Do not hide recurring costs until after approval.

Introduce them clearly but keep the explanation simple.

### Suggested wording

# 8. Do Not Make "Free" the Main Selling Point

A website that is described as "completely free" creates future
problems.

The Principal may later ask:

-   Why is there an email cost?
-   Why does the domain need renewal?
-   Who owns the account?
-   Who will maintain the website?
-   Why does the developer need access?
-   Why is a new service required?
-   What happens if the developer leaves?

Instead, use:

> **"Community-supported development with school-owned
> infrastructure."**

That phrase communicates both generosity and professionalism.

# 11. The "Low-Cost Entry" Strategy

The initial proposal can deliberately minimise financial resistance.

### Phase 1 --- Demonstration

**Past pupil contribution**

-   Prototype
-   Design
-   Initial development
-   Initial setup

### Phase 2 --- Institutional approval

**School approves**

-   Website concept
-   Domain
-   Official content
-   Digital ownership
-   Workspace
-   Administration structure

### Phase 3 --- Launch

**School pays only approved recurring infrastructure costs.**

### Phase 4 --- Future development

Additional features can be proposed only when there is a genuine

This avoids asking the Principal to approve a large financial commitment
before seeing the value.

# 12. Do Not Personally Absorb Permanent Recurring Costs

If you personally pay:

-   Domain
-   Workspace
-   Mobile data
-   Software subscriptions
-   Hosting
-   Other services

the school may eventually assume these are permanently free.

That creates an unhealthy expectation.

Instead:

> **One-time development contribution ≠ permanent operating subsidy.**

Your contribution can be generous without becoming an indefinite
personal obligation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. Mobile Data and Personal Expenses

Your personal mobile data, electricity, computer time and working hours
should not be presented as school expenses unless separately agreed.

For internal planning, recognise them as your contribution.

For example:

> "Initial development and administrative support are being contributed
> by a past pupil."

There is no need to itemise every personal expense to the Principal.

The important thing is that the school understands which third-party
services it must maintain.

-   Approve photographs for publication
-   Pay domain renewal
-   Pay Workspace subscription
-   Maintain authorised payment method
-   Nominate an official contact person
-   Approve major changes

### Developer/past pupil responsibilities

-   Initial configuration
-   Initial deployment

-   Provide reasonable training
-   Maintain documentation
-   Follow school-approved branding/content

### Joint responsibilities

-   Obtain appropriate permissions
-   Verify official information
-   Maintain account security
-   Approve major changes before publication

# 17. Suggested Agreement Clause --- Contribution

> **The Developer/Past Pupil agrees to contribute the initial website
> design, development and setup as a community contribution to the
> school, subject to the agreed project scope. This contribution does
> not include an obligation to permanently finance third-party
> subscriptions, domain renewals, software licences, hosting charges or
> other external service costs.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. Suggested Agreement Clause --- Recurring Services

> **The School agrees to maintain the recurring third-party services
> required for the approved digital platform, including domain
> registration and institutional email services. Such charges shall be
> paid directly by the School where practicable and shall not be treated
> as development fees payable to the Developer.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. Suggested Agreement Clause --- No Hidden Charges

> **No additional paid service will be introduced as a compulsory
> project requirement without informing the School and obtaining
> appropriate approval. Where an optional paid service is proposed, its
> purpose and expected cost will be explained before purchase.**

This clause builds trust.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. Suggested Agreement Clause --- Ownership

> **The School shall retain ownership of its domain, official digital
> accounts, approved content, photographs supplied by the School, and
> institutional records. The Developer may retain appropriate rights in
> original reusable software components unless otherwise agreed in
> writing.**

The exact intellectual-property arrangement should be reviewed before
signing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 21. Suggested Agreement Clause --- Access

> **The School shall maintain appropriate institutional ownership of
> critical accounts. The Developer may be granted
> administrator/developer access where required for technical support.
> Access shall be removed or modified when no longer required.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 22. Suggested Agreement Clause --- Termination

> **Either party may discontinue the ongoing support arrangement with
> reasonable written notice. Discontinuation of support shall not affect
> the School's ownership of its domain and institutional accounts. The
> parties shall cooperate to provide a reasonable handover of available
> technical documentation and access.**

# 30. The Key Psychological Principle

Do not try to manipulate the Principal.

Instead, make the decision **easy to understand**.

A good proposal answers five questions:

### 1. Why?

Because the school needs a professional digital identity.

### 2. What?

A modern official website and digital platform.

### 3. Who?

School owns it; past pupil provides initial development support.

### 4. How much?

Low initial cost, with clearly identified recurring third-party costs.

### 5. What happens later?

Ownership, administration, renewal and maintenance are documented.

When these five questions are clear, the Principal can make a confident
decision without pressure.

The strongest proposal is not:

> **"I will build the website for free."**

It is:

> **"I am willing to contribute the initial professional development
